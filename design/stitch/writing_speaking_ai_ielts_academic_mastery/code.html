It is often argued that high school students should be mandated to participate in voluntary community activities as a formal prerequisite for graduation. In my opinion, I completely agree with this viewpoint because such engagement not only cultivates civic responsibility among teenagers but also equips them with indispensable practical capabilities.

First of all, community work plays an essential role in broadening youngsters' social perspective. When adolescents engage in charitable initiatives, they are exposed to the daily struggles of vulnerable populations. Consequently, this involvement fosters empathy and a profound sense of civic duty, preventing them from developing self-centred mindsets. Furthermore, engaging in communal activities helps alleviate the burden placed on local governments and fosters social cohesion throughout the neighbourhood.

On the other hand, compulsory volunteer programmes provide a valuable environment for students to acquire interpersonal skills.
